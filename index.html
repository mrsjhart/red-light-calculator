2. Your device

Label wattage is wall power, not light output. It does not change the session time.
Use optical output (mW of light), not the electrical watts on the plug.
Intensity falls quickly with distance; closer = shorter time.
How do I find my real irradiance?
  • Look for "irradiance" or "power density" in mW/cm² at a stated distance in the manual or a third-party lab report. Ask the seller for one.
  • Electrical watts (e.g. "45 W") are power drawn from the wall, not light output, and can't be converted directly.
  • For lasers, find the optical output in mW and the spot/aperture size; the calculator divides mW by area.
  • Or measure it with a solar power meter like the Tenmars TM-206 and correct the reading below.

Safety checklist

  • Eye protection for people; never aim at eyes (human or animal). Cover/shield animal eyes when working near the head.
  • Check skin and coat for heat every 2–3 minutes; extra caution on dark coats/skin. Stop if warm to the touch or the animal/person is uneasy.
  • Avoid: laser over open growth plates in young animals; over known tumors/cancer; pregnant abdomen; thyroid area; active bleeding; after photosensitizing meds or topicals.
  • Vet/doctor approval first for unknown lumps, fever, infection, or anything unexplained.
  • Stop if discomfort increases. More light is not better — too much can reduce the effect (biphasic dose response).

How it works & sources

Time (s) = target dose (J/cm²) ÷ (irradiance mW/cm² ÷ 1000). Target dose starts from a conservative range by tissue depth (superficial ~4, moderate ~6, deep ~8 J/cm²), then is lowered for higher discomfort, recent concerns, young/senior, small size and dark coats, and modestly raised only for thick coats (light loss). Hard caps: 10 J/cm² superficial/moderate, 12 deep; 20 min per area. If you don't enter your device's irradiance, a conservative generic estimate is used — find or measure your real value. Electrical watts are not light output. Cross-check: WALT's 780–860 nm laser table uses about 4–16 J per site (minimum ~4 J per point), with a ±50% therapeutic window, and advises reducing ~30% once things settle; the laser energy-per-spot output here falls in that range.
